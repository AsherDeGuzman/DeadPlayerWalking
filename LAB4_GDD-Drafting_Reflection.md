Reflection

a. Does your high concept promise something your core loop actually delivers? Where is the gap, if any?

Our high concept generally matches what our core loop delivers because the main gameplay mechanics support the experience we intended to create. The player can perform the main actions and experience the basic gameplay idea we planned. However, there is still a small gap because our prototype is not yet as complete or polished as the high concept suggests. Some features, visuals, and interactions are still basic. Since we focused on building the core mechanics in Godot, the full experience of the high concept will need more development and refinement.

b. Which formal element was hardest to pin down for your game, and why?

The hardest formal element for our group to determine was the rules and challenges of the game. We had to make sure that the rules were clear while still making the gameplay interesting and manageable to develop. Finding the right balance between difficulty and simplicity was challenging because we wanted the player to have enough challenge without making the game frustrating. Working with Godot also helped us understand which ideas were practical to implement within the time available.

c. What did a peer's read-around make you see that you had missed?

A peer's feedback helped us realize that some parts of our game were not as clear to a new player as they were to us. Since we worked on the game ourselves, we already understood how the mechanics were supposed to work, so we sometimes overlooked areas that could confuse someone playing it for the first time. Their feedback showed us the importance of clear instructions, feedback, and understandable objectives. It encouraged us to look at the game from the player's perspective rather than only from the developer's perspective.
