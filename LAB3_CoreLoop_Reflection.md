Reflection

a. What is the single mechanic at the heart of your loop, and why is it the right one to prototype first?

The main mechanic at the heart of our game loop is player movement and interaction with the game environment. We chose to prototype this first because it is the foundation of how players experience the game. Using Godot, we were able to test the movement, controls, and basic interactions before adding more complex features. Making sure that the core mechanic feels responsive and understandable helped us build a stronger foundation for the rest of the game.

b. Where does the loop currently feel flat, and what one change would most improve the feedback?

The loop currently feels a little flat because the player's actions do not always have enough immediate feedback or noticeable consequences. Some interactions can feel repetitive because there are limited visual or audio responses. One change that would improve the feedback is adding clearer visual and sound effects when the player performs an important action, such as interacting with an object, completing an objective, or encountering something in the environment. This would make the game feel more responsive and engaging.

c. What did you deliberately leave out to keep this build minimal?

To keep our prototype manageable, we deliberately left out more advanced features such as complex enemy AI, detailed levels, extensive animations, a large number of assets, and additional game modes. We focused on creating and testing the core gameplay loop in Godot first. This allowed our group to concentrate on whether the basic mechanics worked before spending time on features that could be added later.
